End-of-Turn Detection Assignment

This project predicts whether a pause in speech corresponds to an End-of-Turn (EOT) or a Hold using only acoustic information available before the pause. The solution follows the causality constraint by never using future information such as pause duration or speech after the pause.

Approach

The starter implementation extracted only simple prosodic statistics and trained a Logistic Regression classifier. My work focused on engineering additional prosodic features that better capture conversational turn-taking behaviour while keeping the model lightweight and fully causal.

Feature Engineering

The final system uses 23 handcrafted acoustic features.

Category Features
Global Energy Mean, standard deviation, last frame energy, energy slope
Local Energy Mean, standard deviation, last-frame energy and energy slope over the last 300 ms before the pause
Global Pitch Mean F0, standard deviation, last voiced F0 and pitch slope
Local Pitch Statistics computed only over the final continuous voiced region before the pause
Voice Activity Voiced ratio, speech duration and pause index
Final Syllable Lengthening Last voiced run length, average previous run length, lengthening ratio and fraction of the utterance occupied by the last voiced region

Model

The extracted features are standardized using StandardScaler and classified using a LogisticRegression model with class_weight="balanced".

Experiments

Experiment Observation
Starter baseline Simple energy and pitch statistics.

Challenges

  • Maintaining strict causality by avoiding future pause information.
  • Designing informative features using only pre-pause audio.
  • Pitch estimation is noisy during weakly voiced speech.
  • Balancing detection accuracy with low interruption rate.

Future Improvements

  • Train a stronger gradient boosting model (XGBoost or LightGBM).
  • Incorporate additional speaking-rate and rhythm features.
  • Use robust pitch extraction such as CREPE or Praat.
  • Train a sequence model that captures temporal evolution of prosody.
  • Evaluate on larger multilingual conversational datasets.

Conclusion

The final system significantly improves over the starter implementation by introducing richer prosodic representations while remaining lightweight, interpretable and fully causal. The feature engineering process demonstrated that conversational cues such as local pitch behaviour and final-syllable lengthening are valuable indicators of end-of-turn prediction.
